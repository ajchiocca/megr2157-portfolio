#About Me
My name is Antonio Chiocca, although I often go by AJ Chiocca, and I am a mechanical engineering student who is motivated by curiosity, creativity, and the opportunity to turn ideas into physical designs. I enjoy pursuing projects that interest me personally because working on a problem that I find meaningful motivates me to understand down to its physics. I enjoy CAD modeling and other engineering work where I can develop an idea, evaluate how it functions, and then refine.
<br>
<br>
I usually participate in group projects and collaborative work, however I often prefer to initially work through problems independently. Working through a topic myself forces me to develop my own understanding rather than relying on an approach different from mine. This has helped me become more confident when explaining my reasoning and making decisions in both engineering projects and coursework. I also recognize the importance of collaboration and learning how to communicate my ideas when working with other engineers.
<br>
<br>
My interest in mechanical engineering began with interests I developed long before college, particularly spacecraft, LEGO, and Minecraft. Although these hobbies are very different from engineering coursework, they all gave me opportunities to create something from an initial idea and make decisions about how it should function. LEGO and Minecraft especially taught me to approach problems creatively, experiment with different solutions, and build something that was sufficient in my eyes. Mechanical engineering provides a more structured version of that process, where I will be given a task with specifications and requirements, then to be modeled and machined. 
<br>
<br>
As I progress through the mechanical engineering program, I am becoming an engineer with more broader technical knowledge and the ability to apply that knowledge to problems I'm unfamiliar with. My goal is to continue developing the analytical, design, and communication skills necessary to turn ideas into valid engineering solutions.
<br>
<br>
Defending an engineering decision means evaluating the facts, constraints, and trade-offs to justify a final decision. I currently understand the general process of making and explaining a decision, but I do not yet have enough experience as a mechanical engineer to confidently defend engineering decisions at a higher level. Developing this ability to better defend engineering decisions requires experience applying engineering principles to real design problems, evaluating alternatives, and explaining why one solution is more appropriate than another decision.
